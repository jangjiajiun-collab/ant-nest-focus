how it's drawn

Render. The scene is drawn by three.js (r160) into a small nearest-filtered target (about 420 px on the short side, finer than the original 480×300) with no anti-aliasing, then scaled up with texelFetch. An orthographic camera sits at 31° elevation and snaps to quarter-turn azimuths. A depth pass darkens any pixel more than 0.5 world units in front of a neighbour.

Palette. Every pixel is matched to one of 64 fixed colours in OKLab, after a 4×4 Bayer dither nudges its lightness. Nothing outside this palette ever reaches the screen:

Why the nest works this way. Walter Tschinkel cast and excavated nests of the Florida harvester ant (Pogonomyrmex badius). Nest depth, chamber count and chamber area increase with colony size, so deeper chambers here need more workers and cost more food. Seeds are stored deep and brood sits low, while foragers work near the top (Tschinkel 1999, Ecological Entomology 24:222; Tschinkel 2004, J. Insect Science 4:21).

The loop. A task becomes a glowing seed. 25 focused minutes is one foraging trip; if you stay, the colony carries the seed to its deepest chamber and you earn food. If you drift, the seed dims and the ants walk home empty-handed. Nothing is ever lost.
